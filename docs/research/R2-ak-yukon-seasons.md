# R2 Fact Sheet: Alaska State and Yukon Placer Rules, Seasons, and Water

Prepared 2026-10-04 for Gold Mining Tycoon system designers. Figures carry the year they apply to.
Confidence tags: **[V]** = found in an agency or primary source during this research (often via search-engine
extracts, because direct fetches of dnr.alaska.gov, yukon.ca, ecfr.gov were blocked); **[S]** = secondary source
(press, industry); **[M]** = from model knowledge and not re-verified in this session. Currency: USD unless
"C$". Conversion used: C$1 = US$0.72 (2025 season average implied by YGS: US$3,476 / C$4,811).

Unit conversions used throughout:
- 1 g/m3 = 0.7646 g/yd3 = **0.0246 oz(t)/yd3**. 0.01 oz/yd3 = 0.41 g/m3. 0.2 g/m3 = 0.0049 oz/yd3; 0.5 = 0.0123; 1.0 = 0.0246; 2.0 = 0.049.
- 1 US gpm = 5.45 m3/day (24 h). 1 Imperial gal = 1.20 US gal. 1 m3 = 1.308 yd3. 1 ha = 2.471 acres.

## Most important numbers (top 10)

1. **Alaska state claim rental (rental year starts noon Sept 1):** per 40-acre quarter-quarter MTRSC claim $40 (claim age 0-5 yr), $85 (6-10 yr), $205 (11+ yr); 160-acre quarter-section claims pay 4x ($160 / $340 / $820; first-year QS bill $165). CPI-indexed every 10 years (last 2019; next ~2029). **[V, rates from 2019 regulation]**
2. **Alaska annual labor:** $100 per 40-acre claim, $400 per 160-acre claim; labor year ends noon Sept 1; Statement of Annual Labor recorded within 90 days (**Nov 30**) or the claim is abandoned by law; cash in lieu allowed max **5 consecutive years**. **[V]**
3. **Alaska state take on profit:** mining license tax 0% to $40k net, 3% $40-50k, $1,500 + 5% to $100k, **$4,000 + 7% above $100k**; new operations **exempt 3.5 years** from first production; plus **3% of net income production royalty** on state land (rental paid is credited against royalty). **[V]**
4. **Alaska reclamation bonding:** plan and bond required at **5+ acres** disturbed (plan also triggered by **50,000 yd3** moved in a year); placer bond **$750/acre**; Statewide Bond Pool = **$150/acre/yr: $112.50 refundable deposit + $37.50 nonrefundable fee**; annual reclamation statement due **Dec 31**; bond renewal due **Apr 1**. **[V]**
5. **Yukon claim:** creek claim 500 ft along baseline x up to 1,000 ft each side (about **23 acres / 9.3 ha**); **$10** to record; renew yearly with **$200 representation work** per claim (excess credit up to $800/claim/yr, banked in $200 steps); grouping lets work on one claim count for all. **[V]**
6. **Yukon classes:** Class 1 = notification only (25-day review); Class 2 = YESAB assessment then notification; Class 3 = over Class 2 limits but water **<= 300 m3/day** and no waste to a watercourse; **Class 4 = > 300 m3/day, or any waste deposit, or any in-stream work** -> YESAB + Yukon Water Board water licence + land use approval (5- or 10-year terms). 300 m3/day is only **~55 US gpm continuous**, so essentially every production wash plant is Class 4. **[V]**
7. **Yukon timelines:** Class 4 YESAB assessment **8-10 months**; decision document legally 30 days, **~60 days average** for placer; Water Board licensing follows. 2024-25 backlog left some miners waiting **> 1 year** and forced temporary licence extensions (May 2025). **[V/S]**
8. **Yukon royalty:** **C$0.375 per troy oz** (2.5% of a fixed C$15/oz value, set 1906), charged only on gold **exported** from the territory. 2025: ~C$33,300 collected on C$401-449M of placer gold (**< 0.01%**). **[V/S]**
9. **Seasons:** Klondike sluicing typically mid/late May to late Sept or early Oct (**~18-22 weeks**, up to ~24 in a mild fall); Yukon River breakup at Dawson ~early May (trend ~7 days earlier over 120 yr), freeze-up mid-Nov; Fairbanks first fall freeze ~Sept 15 (range Aug 24-Sept 23); Alaska placer seasons **90-150 days (13-21 weeks)**. **[V/S]**
10. **Water:** stationary trommel/screen + sluice plants use **650-2,000 US gal per yd3** (~**11-33 gpm per yd3/h**; field data cluster at **10-15 gpm per yd3/h**); settling ponds must hold sludge equal to **25-40% of the pay volume** processed; Alaska aquatic-life turbidity limit **25 NTU above natural** (5 NTU for drinking-water use), settleable solids **0.2 ml/L**; Yukon settleable-solids compliance levels **~1.2-1.5 ml/L** in low-sensitivity streams, near zero in high-sensitivity ones. **[V]**

## 1. Alaska STATE mining claims (state land, not federal)

**Claim types and sizes [V/M]**
- MTRSC (Meridian-Township-Range-Section-Claim) locations, defined by the public land survey grid: **quarter-quarter section about 40 acres** or **quarter section about 160 acres**. [V]
- "Traditional" staked claims: up to 40 acres (1,320 x 1,320 ft), corner-posted. [M] MTRSC claims are described by grid position and need far less fieldwork. Prospecting sites and leasehold locations also exist. [V]
- Locating: post the location notice; record the certificate of location in the District Recorder's office **within 45 days**; pay the **first rental within 45 days** ($40 per QQ, $165 per QS). That first payment covers posting date to the next Sept 1. Recording fees are separate and change over time. [V]
- Game implication: state claims come in 40/160-acre blocks (vs 20-acre federal placer claims). One 160-acre state claim holds the ground of 8 federal claims at a fraction of the paperwork.

**Annual rental (11 AAC 86.221; AS 38.05.211) [V]**

| Years since first located | Per 40-acre QQ claim | Per 160-acre QS claim (4x) |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | $40 | $160 (first year billed $165) |
| 6-10 | $85 | $340 |
| 11+ | $205 | $820 |

- The 2000-statute base rates were $20 / $40 / $100 per QQ (still on some old forms). The 2019 CPI adjustment, effective for the rental year starting Sept 1, 2019, set the current rates. DNR must re-index every 10 years. [V]
- Rental (mining) year runs noon Sept 1 to noon Sept 1. Rent for the coming year is paid **between Sept 1 and Nov 30**, alongside the labor filing. Not paying means statutory abandonment. Statute language and bills describe curing an abandonment by paying a **penalty equal to one year's rent**, but whether and how far that cure applies today was not verified. [V/M]
- Rental is **credited against that year's production royalty**, so a producing claim effectively pays no separate rent. [V]
- Game implication: rent climbs with claim age (5x by year 11), which pushes players to drop or work ground and gives old family claims (Inheritor start) a higher holding cost. The Sept 1 and Nov 30 dates belong on the compliance calendar.

**Annual labor (AS 38.05.210) [V]**
- **$100 of labor per 40-acre location; $400 per 160-acre MTRSC location** per labor year (ends noon Sept 1).
- Statement (or Affidavit) of Annual Labor recorded within **90 days, by Nov 30**. A late filing means **automatic abandonment** (AS 38.05.265).
- **Cash in lieu:** pay the state the value of the required labor, for **no more than 5 consecutive years**. DNR's form says cash in lieu must be paid **by Sept 1**. [V]
- Labor beyond the requirement can be banked for later years (about 4 years). [M]
- Game implication: labor works like the federal Small Miner's Waiver but has no claim-count cap. Model it as "$X of qualifying work per claim per year, or pay cash, with cash limited to 5 years in a row," then force real work or production.

**Alaska mining license tax (AS 43.65, Dept. of Revenue) [V]**
- Tax on net income from Alaska mining property (depletion allowed):
  - $0-40,000: 0%; $40,001-50,000: 3%; $50,001-100,000: $1,500 + 5% of excess over $50k; over $100,000: **$4,000 + 7% of excess over $100k**.
- **New-operation exemption: 3.5 years after production begins** (still in AS 43.65.010; start date set by an Affidavit of Initial Production). A 2016 bill (HB 4005) proposed cutting it to 2 years, and no enactment was found. [V]
- Annual mining license required; returns for calendar-year filers due **April 30**; filed on Revenue Online. [V]
- Effective rate examples: $150k net pays $7,500 (5.0%); $500k net pays $32,000 (6.4%); $1M net pays $67,000 (6.7%).
- Statewide mining license tax revenue swings from about **-$1M to $50M+ per year**; recent average about $28.5M, forecast $33-36M (2025). Almost all of it comes from big lode mines. [S]
- Game implication: small placer outfits under $40k net pay nothing, which suits a Bootstrapper. The 3.5-year holiday rewards a new company that starts production quickly. Progressive brackets fit cleanly in a data table.

**Alaska production royalty (AS 38.05.212; 11 AAC 86.760-796) [V]**
- **3% of net income** (net income as computed for the mining license tax) on minerals from **state land**. File the license tax return first, then the royalty return. Rental paid is credited against the royalty. [V]
- Unverified: whether the 3.5-year exemption also shelters the royalty. The royalty uses AS 43.65 net income, and DNR's form refers to AS 43.65.010. Treat as uncertain. [M]
- Exploration incentive credits may offset up to 50% of royalty liability. [V]
- Combined state take on state land at $500k net is about 6.4% + 3% = **about 9.4% of net income**, before federal income tax and any Alaska corporate income tax (C-corps, top rate about 9.4%). [M] Alaska has no personal income tax. [M]
- Game implication: unlike a gross royalty, a net-income royalty costs nothing in a loss year. It is a good contrast with private lease royalties of 5-25% of gross.

**APMA: Application for Permits to Mine in Alaska [V]**
- One annual packet covers placer, hardrock exploration, and suction dredging, and is routed to up to **~12 state and federal agencies**. **$100 application fee** for placer. [V]
- What it covers:
  - **DNR Division of Mining, Land & Water:** reclamation plan approval (AS 27.19), land-use authorizations on state land, Temporary Water Use Authorization or water rights (AS 46.15), and cross-country travel authorizations (apply **60-90 days ahead**).
  - **DEC:** for qualifying operations the APMA serves as the **Notice of Intent** under the **APDES General Permit AKG370000** for mechanical placer miners (gravity separation only).
  - **ADF&G Habitat:** Fish Habitat Permit under AS 16.05.871 (anadromous waters) and AS 16.05.841 (fish passage). Typical terms: seasonal in-stream windows, 1/8-inch intake screens, fish-passage flows.
  - Copies go to BLM for federal land and the Army Corps for 404 wetlands. [V]
- **Multi-year approvals: 1-10 year terms**. Some program material says up to 5 years for placer. Multi-year holders still file yearly: **bond renewal and fees by Apr 1**, signed **reclamation plan or Letter of Intent by Dec 31**, and **Annual Reclamation Statement by Dec 31**. [V]
- Processing: first come, first served, and slower during the summer field season. No official day count was found. Expect about 30-90 days for a clean placer APMA and longer with public notice or anadromous streams. [M]
- Game implication: one combined permit pipeline with sub-approvals that can fail on their own (fish habitat, discharge, water). A multi-year approval is a sellable asset. An application filed in May waits behind the summer rush.

**Reclamation and bonding (AS 27.19; 11 AAC 97) [V]**
- Reclamation is required for all operations. A **reclamation plan** is required if this year's disturbance is **>= 5 acres**, OR **>= 50,000 yd3** is moved, OR **cumulative unreclaimed disturbance >= 5 acres**. Below these thresholds a Letter of Intent to Reclaim suffices. [V]
- **Bond required at >= 5 acres.** Placer bond rate **$750/acre**, unless a third-party reclamation estimate is lower. [V] At about $5-6k/acre, real costs run far above $750 (see the Idaho comparison below), so the pool is a subsidy. [S]
- **Statewide Bond Pool (11 AAC 97.425):** voluntary, for operators that use no chemicals and have no acid-rock-drainage potential. **$150/acre/yr = $112.50 refundable deposit (15% of $750) + $37.50 nonrefundable fee (5%)**. The deposit is refundable once the acreage is reclaimed. Since 2015 the pool can also be used on BLM land in Alaska (cooperative agreement). No reclamation approval takes effect until pool fees are paid. [V]
- Other bond forms: surety bond, personal bond backed by a letter of credit, CD, cash, or gold deposit. [V]
- Game implication: the bond pool makes Alaska cheap to enter. A 20-acre pit costs $3,000/yr in pool payments, $750 of which is gone for good. A direct bond for 20 acres is $15,000 in surety or cash. Forfeiting pool participation damages regulator standing.

**Alaska water rights (11 AAC 93.035) [V]**
- A water right or Temporary Water Use Authorization (TWUA, **<= 5 years**) is required to use more than **5,000 gal from one source in one day**, more than **500 gal/day for more than 10 days a year**, or more than **30,000 gal/day non-consumptive (0.05 cfs)**. Unpermitted users have no priority and can be curtailed. [V]
- Any wash plant blows past these thresholds within minutes, so every producing operation needs a TWUA or permit. It is applied for through the APMA.
- Game implication: water authorization is a gating sub-permit. Its 5-year expiry is a renewal event.

**Alaska discharge (DEC APDES AKG370000) [V]**
- Covers process wastewater, dewatering water, and drainage from open-cut placer mines and mechanical dredges using gravity separation.
- Monitoring if discharging: flow, **settleable solids, turbidity, arsenic**. Daily inspection log required.
- Settleable solids limit **0.2 ml/L** (federal placer effluent guideline, 40 CFR 440 subpart M). A **500-ft mixing zone** may be authorized; without one, turbidity standards must be met at the outfall.
- Alaska water quality standards (18 AAC 70): turbidity **<= 5 NTU above natural** for drinking-water use (where natural <= 50 NTU), and **<= 25 NTU above natural** for aquatic life.
- The federal guideline expects recycling. New placer sources are effectively zero-discharge except storm or excess water. Mines processing **< 1,500 yd3/yr** are exempt from the guideline. [M]
- Game implication: a turbidity exceedance is the classic inspection violation. Pond capacity and recycle rate should be player-managed variables tied to violation probability.

**Alaska compliance calendar (game-ready)**
- Apr 1: bond pool renewal and fees.
- Apr 30: mining license tax return and production royalty return.
- Sept 1 (noon): labor year ends, rental year starts, cash in lieu due.
- Nov 30: Statement of Annual Labor recorded and rental paid; missing either abandons the claim.
- Dec 31: Annual Reclamation Statement plus next season's plan or Letter of Intent.

## 2. Yukon: Placer Mining Act and Waters Act regime

**Claims (Placer Mining Act, SY 2003 c.13) [V]**
- **Creek claim:** up to **500 ft along the baseline x 1,000 ft on each side** (<= 2,000 ft wide), which is 1,000,000 sq ft, about **23 acres / 9.3 ha**. Non-creek claim geometries differ and were not verified. [V/M]
- Anyone 18 or older may stake. Apply within the **travel time: 10 days plus 1 day per 10 miles** (16.1 km) from the Mining Recorder. **Application fee $10 per claim.** [V]
- **Term: 1 year** from recording. Renewal requires **$200 of representation work per claim per year** (Schedule of Representation Work sets rates for miner-like work), plus a small renewal fee (historically $10). [V]
- **Excess credit:** work beyond $200 can be banked, **max $800 per claim per year**, applied in $200 steps. [V]
- **Grouping:** claims in one operation can be grouped so work anywhere in the group renews all of them. Fees: **$5/claim for the first 10, $1 for each additional claim**. Large groups need proof that they form one operation. [V]
- No placer cash-in-lieu option was found. Unlike quartz claims, placer claims appear to require real work. [M, unverified]
- **Prospecting lease:** up to about 5 miles of creek for 1 year, then staked into claims. [M; guidelines exist]
- **Free-entry and consultation:** *Ross River Dena Council v. Yukon* (YKCA 2012; Supreme Court of Canada leave denied) found a duty to notify and consult before staking and Class 1 work in that territory. Yukon then amended both mining Acts and brought in a Class 1 notification regime, now applied in designated areas covering about 47% of the territory. [V]
- Game implication: Yukon claims are small (about 23 acres), cheap ($10), and renewed by work rather than fees. An idle claimant must spend about $200/claim/yr in real work. Claims renew on staggered recording anniversaries rather than one statewide date.

**Operation classes (Placer Mining Land Use Regulation, YOIC 2003/59) [V unless marked]**

| Class | Trigger and limits (examples) | Approval path |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fuel <= 5,000 L; <= 250 person-days per camp; trenching <= 400 m3 per claim (1,200 m3 per 3-claim group); ditching <= 250 m3; stripping <= 600 m2 per claim per year | **Class 1 notification**; once complete, a **25-day review** by affected First Nations and stakeholders. No YESAB. |
| 2 | Above Class 1 limits, e.g. fuel up to 40,000 L (<= 10,000 L per container), > 250 person-days, > 10 people at once | **YESAB assessment**, then a **Class 2 notification** to the Chief of Mining Land Use after the decision document |
| 3 | Exceeds Class 2 limits; water use **<= 300 m3/day**; **no waste deposited in a watercourse** | YESAB plus operating plan approval (Mining Land Use) [M on exact paperwork] |
| 4 | Exceeds Class 2 limits AND (**> 300 m3/day** water, OR **deposit of waste** in a watercourse, OR **any in-stream works**, including water crossings) | **YESAB + Yukon Water Board water licence (placer/Type B) + Class 4 mining land use approval**, both issued by the Board (EMR has delegated land-use authority to it) |

- Water use **under 300 m3/day** without a licence still requires a **Schedule 3 Notice of Water Use without a Licence** filed with the Board. [V]
- Class 4 land-use approval fees: **C$500 for a 10-year term, C$250 for 5 years**. The Board routinely grants 10-year terms. [V]
- Key arithmetic: 300 m3/day = 79,000 US gal/day = **55 gpm over 24 h, or about 130 gpm over a 10-h sluice day**. A 50 yd3/h plant needs about 500-750 gpm, so **any real production plant is Class 4**. Classes 1-3 cover test pits, small test plants, and exploration. [derived]
- Placer mining accounts for **93% of all permitted water use in Yukon**. [S]
- Game implication: a clean 4-tier ladder keyed to water rate and in-stream work. A player trying to dodge Class 4 can only run a tiny recirculating test plant.

**YESAB and Water Board process and timelines [V/S]**
- Placer projects in the Klondike go to the **Dawson Designated Office**. A Designated Office evaluation runs **2-5 weeks of public "seeking views"** once the application is judged adequate, then **2-5 weeks to write the recommendation**. Executive Committee screenings are for large projects and are rare for placer. [V/M]
- **Class 4 placer at YESAB: 8-10 months** (YESAB figure, 2024). The decision document is legislated at **30 days** but averages **~60 days** for placer. [S]
- Yukon Water Board: applicants often file with YESAB and the Board at the same time. The Board can screen for adequacy but cannot start public comment until the decision document exists. Affected First Nations, departments, and the public can then intervene. [V]
- Realistic new-project total: **about 12-18 months** from filing to a licence in hand; up to 2 years with information requests or hearings. [M, derived]
- **Backlog, 2024-26:** **10-year licence cycles** bunch renewals into the same years. In 2025 about **50 placer projects** with expired or expiring approvals were waiting. One miner waited 11+ months, against an expected 8-10. In **May 2025** the territory amended regulations to allow **temporary licence extensions**. By mid-2026 it had issued **41 placer decision documents** that year, about one every second day. [S]
- High-profile refusals happen: YESAB recommended against, and both governments rejected (2024), a placer mine on a creek used for drinking water west of Whitehorse. [S]
- Game implication: Yukon permitting runs as a staged pipeline (adequacy, seeking views, recommendation, decision document, Board licence) with stochastic durations. Rejection or "recommend against" is a real outcome, and renewal waves create shared backlog delays for all players and AI competitors.

**First Nations [V/M]**
- All YESAB placer files go to affected First Nations for views. Class 1 notifications get a 25-day First Nations review. The Klondike lies in Tr'ondek Hwech'in traditional territory. [M]
- Wetlands are the hot-button issue. *A Policy for the Stewardship of Yukon's Wetlands* (2024) lets placer mining continue in wetlands. The Board may require a **progressive wetland reclamation plan** before disturbance, and an Indian River wetlands hearing and conditions have occurred. [V/S]
- Game implication: tag claims with a "sensitivity" value (wetland, drinking water, salmon). It raises rejection odds, condition severity, and timelines.

**Royalty [V/S]**
- **C$0.375 per troy oz**: 2.5% of a fixed C$15/oz value, set in 1906 and unchanged since. Levied on gold **shipped out of Yukon** (dust or bars) and paid to the Yukon government. Gold kept or sold inside the territory is not subject. Exporting without paying is an offence: fine up to C$1,000 and/or up to 3 years. [V]
- About US$0.27/oz. At C$6,000/oz gold the rate is **0.006%** of value. 2025: about C$33,300 collected against C$449M of revenue (CBC) or C$401M (YGS valuation). [S]
- Reform: a draft framework for new minerals legislation (2025) proposed modernizing royalties. The Yukon Party government elected Nov 2025 has not committed to it. [S]
- Game implication: per the brief, "cost is in time, not tax." Use a flat per-oz charge at export, plus an optional "royalty reform" event risk in later game years.

**Fish habitat and sediment (DFO Fisheries Act authorizations via Yukon Placer Secretariat) [V]**
- The **Fish Habitat Management System for Yukon Placer Mining** (about 2008) uses **watershed-based authorizations**, for example Indian, Klondike, Fortymile, Sixtymile, Stewart, McQuesten, White, and Yukon River North/South.
- Each authorization has a **habitat suitability map**: High, Moderate-High, Moderate-Moderate, Moderate-Low, Low, and Water Quality Objective zones. Each has its own risk matrix and **sediment discharge standards**.
- Standards are measured as **settleable solids (ml/L)**: a design target, an action level, and a compliance level. White River example for Moderate-Low habitat: **design 0.2, action 0.8-1.0, compliance 1.2-1.5 ml/L**. In **High** suitability habitat the objectives aim for **no sediment discharge**. Monitoring covers TSS, settleable solids, and turbidity. Adaptive management means standards are revised on review cycles.
- Game implication: each stream segment carries a habitat class that sets the allowed effluent level. That decides whether a claim needs full recycle (higher capex and ponds) or can discharge.

**Security deposits [V/M]**
- The Chief of Mining Land Use can require security at any time, and Board licences may set security. The federal 2001 water licence security policy uses the RECLAIM cost model. [V]
- No per-hectare placer figure was found. Placer securities are generally modest, from about zero to tens of thousands of C$. Quartz mines carry far larger amounts (Victoria Gold raised to C$104.9M; Minto C$104M). [V for quartz figures; M for placer range]
- 2024 consultation: industry floated alternatives to blanket security, including a **pooled security regime**. [V]
- Game implication: Yukon placer bonds are small but can jump under reform. Model as a low base with an event or difficulty modifier.

## 3. Typical Yukon/Klondike and Interior Alaska operation profile

**Yukon industry scale (YGS Yukon Exploration and Geology overviews) [V]**

| Season | Operations | Crude oz | Revenue | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | n/a | ~78,000 (Indian R. 36,787 = 47%) | n/a | 6th straight year > 70,000 crude oz |
| 2023 | 146 | n/a | C$143.7M (to Nov 10) | Mild fall, sluicing to end of Oct; Dawson district 89% of output |
| 2024 | 156 | **85,799** | **C$230M** | Record revenue. Dawson 70% / Mayo 21% / Whitehorse 9% of ops. Indian R. 39,907; Klondike R. 14,725 (+28%); West Yukon 12,645; Lower Stewart 11,173; Mayo 3,972 |
| 2025 | **165** | **104,367** (Apr 1-Nov 1) | **C$401M** at avg C$4,811/oz (US$3,476) | Highest since 1997; Dawson 68% / Whitehorse 18% / Mayo 14% of ops; record C$6,114/oz on Oct 20, 2025 |

- **Average per operation: about 550 (2024) to about 630 (2025) crude oz**, roughly 450-510 fine oz. The distribution is heavily skewed: a few large Indian River and Klondike operations produce several thousand to 10,000+ oz, while many family operations produce 100-500 oz. [V for mean; M for distribution]
- **Crude vs fine:** YGS says impurities (Ag, Cu, quartz) average **about 20% of crude mass**, so fineness is about 0.80. [V]
- **Interior Alaska:** about **145 placer mines (2023)** and about 150 (2024 DGGS), with "more than 200" cited for 2024 in trade press. **2018: just under 200 placer mines produced 60,691 oz (about $77M)**, so the average is **about 300 oz per mine**. Statewide gold (mostly lode): about 728k oz (2023), 959k oz (2024), 960k oz (2025). [V/S]
- Game implication: a "typical" northern placer company makes 300-700 oz/yr. 2,000+ oz/yr is a top-decile operator, and 10,000 oz/yr is an elite multi-plant company. Good scenario goals: first 1,000 oz cumulative, then 1,000 oz/yr.

**Grades [M unless noted]**
- Modern Klondike and Interior pay gravel: commonly **0.3-1.5 g/m3 bank** (**0.007-0.037 oz/yd3**). Rich pockets run 3-10+ g/m3 (0.07-0.25 oz/yd3). Grades fall sharply once diluted by stripping. [M]
- With 2025-26 gold above C$6,000/oz, reportedly even "half an ounce per 100 yd3" (**0.005 oz/yd3, about 0.2 g/m3**) ground is commercially viable for efficient large operations. [S]
- Historic hand-mining in the upper Bonanza area returned about 8-12 g/yd3 (0.26-0.39 oz/yd3), which is why the old-timers' tailings are being re-mined. [S]
- These figures match the brief's 0.005-0.03 oz/yd3 range. The economic cutoff moves with the gold price, so model cutoff grade as a price-dependent output, not a constant.

**Stratigraphy, stripping, permafrost [V/M]**
- Dominion Creek (Klondike): **2-16 m of "muck"** (frozen silt, peat, organic detritus), over **3-4 m of Dominion Creek gravel**, over **> 5 m of Ross gravel**. Indian River pay is about **5 ft (1.5 m) thick**. [V]
- Strip ratio (overburden : pay) commonly **3:1 to 10:1** in valley-bottom Klondike ground. Bench and "White Channel" ground differs. [M]
- Practices: strip muck with dozers (D8-D10 class) and excavators. Frozen muck and gravel are ripped and left exposed so sun and air thaw them. Unstripped, the sun thaws only a few feet per summer. Hydraulic monitors and cold-water thawing pipes (historic dredging era) are rare today. [V]
- Operators **strip one season ahead** (late summer and fall) so ground is thawed and drained for the next season's sluicing. Ripped, drained ground thaws on the order of **5-10 cm/day** in mid-summer; this rate was not verified. [M]
- Game implication: add a "thawed inventory" state per cut. Stripping done this year adds thawed pay for next year. Frozen ground cuts digging rate by about 50-80% and raises wear (ripper hours). A cold summer means less thaw.

## 4. Seasons: breakup, freeze-up, sluicing windows

**Klondike / Dawson City (64.1 N) [V/S]**
- **Yukon River breakup at Dawson:** records since **1896**, the longest high-latitude series in North America. From 1896 to 1939, breakup never came in April. The long-term trend is about **7 days earlier**, with ~**May 3** the most likely date as of 2014. **2016 was the earliest on record** (late April). [V]
- **George Black ferry** (Yukon River crossing at Dawson, used by Top of the World Highway traffic): averages **153 days, May 15-Oct 15**. [V]
- **Freeze-up:** frazil (slush) ice from **late October**; the river is usually frozen by **mid-November**. The ice bridge is usable from **late Dec / early Jan**. [V]
- **Dawson normals:** April high 5 / low -10 C; May 14 / 0; Sept 11 / 0; Oct -1 / -9. [S]
- **Sluicing window:** typically **mid/late May to late Sept / early Oct**, about **18-22 weeks**. Some operators sluice and sell gold **before May** (2019); in a mild fall (2023, Oct mean -2.6 C) they sluiced **to the end of October**. YGS counts production from **Apr 1 to Nov 1**. [V]
- Full site season (mobilize, strip, sluice, clean up, demob): about **22-26 weeks**. [M]
- **2022 Klondike River:** ice-jam flooding after near-record heat (about 23 C) and a rain-on-snow event in early June caused washouts and evacuations. **2023:** Klondike streams flowed above the 75th percentile all season, while Whitehorse had its 3rd-driest period on record. [V/S]

**Fortymile / Chicken, Alaska (64.1 N, high plateau) [V/S]**
- Access: **Taylor Highway and Top of the World Highway are unmaintained from mid-October to May**. The Poker Creek-Little Gold border crossing is **closed mid-September to mid-May**. Chicken businesses run about **May 15 to Sept 20**. [V/S]
- Example historic season: **Apr 20-Sept 12** (DGGS report). Most Chicken-area miners run **about June 1 to mid-September** (about 14-16 weeks). [V/M]
- **2004 Taylor Complex fires:** 842,402 acres burned; **46 active placer operations** in the area lost access as the Taylor Highway closed on and off. Chicken was evacuated by convoy to Tok. [S]

**Fairbanks / Circle (64.8 N / 65.8 N) [V/S]**
- **Last spring frost:** 90% chance by May 9, 10% chance as late as May 27 (2026's was May 22). **First fall freeze:** 10% chance by Aug 24, 90% by Sept 23; **typical ~Sept 15** over the last 17 years. Frost-free season **96-126 days**. 2025 had one of the longest growing seasons on record. [V/S]
- Monthly means: May 46.5 F, Jun 58.3, Jul 60.2, Aug 52.8, Sep 42.4. October averages well below freezing. [S/M]
- Alaska placer season length typically **90-150 days (13-21 weeks)**. Circle district runs about 1-2 weeks shorter than Fairbanks, and higher creeks shorter still. [S/M]

**How freeze-up ends a season [M]**
- Wash water freezes in sluice riffles, pump intakes, and supply lines. Settling ponds skin over, then freeze thick enough to block recycle. Thawed stockpiles refreeze into blocks.
- A few nights at -5 to -10 C are workable with night shutdowns, but **sustained daily highs below about -5 C end sluicing**. Final cleanup can run a week or two longer with heat.
- Late-season output is also capped by declining daylight (Dawson loses about 7 min/day in September) and higher fuel use for heat.

**Year-to-year variability (for RNG tuning) [V/M]**
- Breakup date SD about **5-7 days**; first-freeze SD about **7-10 days**. Season length therefore varies about **+/-2-3 weeks** (roughly 16-24 sluicing weeks in the Klondike). [M]
- Territory production swings about 20-30% per year from weather plus price (2023 vs 2024 revenue C$144M vs C$230M). [V]

**Winter roads and trails [V/M]**
- **Alaska tundra travel (state land, North Slope):** opening requires **>= 6 in snow** (coastal) or **9 in** (foothills), and **ground at or below 23 F (-5 C) at 1 ft (30 cm) depth**. Typical openings are mid-December to January; closure comes with the spring thaw in late April or May. Off-road travel permit required. [V]
- Interior placer camps without roads mobilize heavy iron over **winter trails, roughly January-March**, or fly in. A cross-country travel authorization needs **60-90 days lead time**. [V/M]
- Yukon: winter roads and trails to remote creeks, plus the Dawson ice bridge (late Dec to April). Freeze-up and breakup cut access for 4-8 weeks each, in Oct-Dec and Apr-May. [V/M]
- Game implication: access type sets the mobilization window (highway: any time the road is open; seasonal road: May-Oct; winter trail: Jan-Mar only; fly-in: any time, at 5-10x cost). Missing the winter-trail window means no new equipment until next year.

## 5. Lower-latitude placer (NV, AZ, CA, OR, ID, MT)

**Season shapes [M unless noted]**
- **Arizona / Nevada desert:** **Oct-Apr is prime** (summer highs above 100-115 F make crews unproductive and unsafe). Effective season about **30-40 weeks**, limited by water rather than cold. **Arizona monsoon: Jun 15-Sept 30 (peak Jul-Aug)** brings flash floods in washes, lightning, and dust storms. [V for monsoon]
- **California Sierra foothills / Klamath:** the wet season (Nov-Apr) brings high flows and regulatory windows; the dry season (May-Oct) brings water scarcity plus fire season. [M]
- **Oregon / Idaho / Montana mountains:** snow-free access about **May/June to Oct** (about 20-28 weeks). Peak runoff in May-June is too high to work streams; **Aug-Sept low flows** strain water supply. [M]

**Water constraints [V/M]**
- Nevada and Arizona basins are often **fully appropriated**. Nevada's State Engineer can issue temporary changes or waivers in such basins (e.g., Form 4024 mining waiver, **$120 fee**). [V]
- **Trucking water:** a 4,000-gal water truck rents for about **$900-2,000/day (avg about $1,400, 2024-25)**. A 100 yd3/h wet plant at about 15 gpm per yd3/h needs 1,500 gpm (90,000 gal/h, about 22 truckloads/hour). Even at 10% recycle makeup it needs about 2 truckloads/hour. So **desert wet plants need a well and full recycle**, or they **dry-wash**. [V for rental; derived]
- **Dry washing:** material moisture must be **<= about 3%**. Recovery is poor on fine or flaky gold: one side-by-side test got **about 35% more gold wet** than with a dry washer. [V]
- Game implication: desert districts swap "season length" for "water budget." Choices are drill a well (capex plus a water right), recycle ponds, truck water (opex that scales with throughput), or dry-wash (lose fine gold, about 25-40% lower recovery).

**Heat and wildfire closures [V/M]**
- **USFS Industrial Fire Precaution Levels (Region 6, OR/WA; also used in ID):** apply **Apr 1-Oct 31** to industrial and mining operations on National Forest land.
  - All levels: a **1-hour fire watch** after engines shut down.
  - **Level II "partial hoot owl":** spark-emitting work (power saws, welding/cutting, blasting) only **8 pm-1 pm**.
  - **Level III "partial shutdown":** mechanized operations limited to 8 pm-1 pm.
  - **Level IV "general shutdown": all operations prohibited.** [V]
- BLM/USFS **Stage 1/Stage 2 fire restrictions** (NV, AZ, MT, ID): Stage 2 typically bans internal-combustion engine operation off roads, welding, and torches unless a written exemption or waiver is held. [M]
- Game implication: summer fire events cut operating hours (hoot owl: about -40% of daytime hours) or shut the site for weeks (Level IV). Lower-latitude claims trade cold-season risk for fire-season risk.

**State permitting snapshots [V]**
- **California:** **suction dredging is prohibited statewide**. CDFW may not issue permits. **SB 637 (2015)** requires a Water Board waste discharge permit for any motorized suction mining, including highbanking. In Apr 2025 the Karuk Tribe and nine groups sued over CDFW approvals. SMARA reclamation plans are needed above about 1 acre or 1,000 yd3. [V/M]
- **Oregon SB 3 (2017, effective 2021):** bans **motorized in-stream placer mining up to the ordinary high-water line in essential indigenous anadromous salmonid habitat**. Elsewhere it requires DEQ "700-PM" coverage, **<= 200 permits**, suction hose **<= 4 in.**, and no operation 8 pm-8 am within 1,000 ft of residences or campgrounds. [V]
- **Idaho:** an IDL **Dredge & Placer Mining permit is required for more than 0.5 acre** disturbed. Fee **$50 per 10 acres**. Bond = reclamation cost + 10%, **capped by statute at $1,800/acre**, though IDL says real reclamation costs are **$5-6k/acre** (2023 proposals to remove the cap). [V]
- **Montana:** a **Small Miner Exclusion Statement** covers **< 5 acres** total disturbance, roads included. Placer use requires a **$10,000 reclamation bond** (or a lower DEQ-calculated amount), renewed **annually** (expires Dec 31). No equipment may run in a live stream. Larger operations need a full DEQ operating permit. [V]

## 6. Wash plant water requirements and water management

**Process water per yard [V]**

| Method | US gal per yd3 | US gpm per yd3/h |
|---|---|---|
| Rocker (water reused) | 50-100 net | 1-2 |
| Stationary trommel/screen + sluice | **650-2,000** | **11-33** |
| Hydraulicking | 2,000-32,000 | 33-530 |
| Ground sluicing | 22,000-162,000 | 370-2,700 |

- Yukon operator data (Imperial gallons converted to US):
  - 5x24 ft trommel, 50 yd3/h on 400-500 igpm, about **10-12 US gpm per yd3/h**.
  - 85 yd3/h on 900 igpm, about **12.7**.
  - 6-in pump at 1,500 igpm feeding 120 yd3/h, about **15**.
  - 7x40 ft trommel, 100 yd3/h on about 1,500 gpm, about **15**. [V]
- **Design constant: 15 US gpm per yd3/h** (range 10-20 for trommel/sluice plants; 25-35 for clay-rich ground needing scrubbing). [derived]
  - 30 yd3/h starter plant: about **450 gpm**, about 2,450 m3/day over 24 h.
  - 150 yd3/h: about **2,250 gpm**.
  - 300 yd3/h: about **4,500 gpm**.
- Sluice loading guide: **<= about 8 loose yd3/h per foot of sluice width**, or fine gold is lost. [S]

**Recycle and makeup [V/M]**
- Recycle systems typically circulate **about 40% more process water** than once-through systems (higher solids tolerance needed). [V]
- Consumptive loss (makeup) in one documented gravity plant: **about 10% of circulating flow** (300 of 3,000 gpm). Reported recirculation shares run **40-80%** of process water. [V/S]
- Makeup sinks: water held in tailings and pond sludge (largest), seepage, evaporation. Rainfall and groundwater inflow offset some of it. [V/M]
- **Design range: makeup = 10-25% of circulating flow** (low in permafrost valleys with tight ground; high on porous desert gravels). [derived]

**Settling ponds [V]**
- Yukon best practice: ponds sized for **25-40% of the volume of pay gravel processed**, kept with **0.6-1.0 m** of water above the accumulated solids. Valley gradients **> 3%** are impractical for ponds.
- Minimum valley width: **72 m** (4 m overburden), **96 m** (10 m), **120 m** (18 m).
- Forcing effluent through coarse tailings removes up to **97%** of sediment. Pre-settling ponds cut the load about in half. Ponds remove > 0.002-0.02 mm particles well, clays poorly.
- Alaska DEC publishes a Placer Mining Settling Pond Design Handbook (in the APMA packet) with a step-by-step sizing worksheet.
- Example: a 60,000 yd3 pay season needs 15,000-24,000 yd3 of sludge capacity, about 11,500-18,400 m3. At 2 m effective depth that is **about 1.4-2.3 acres of pond**, and the ponds count toward disturbed (bonded) acreage. [derived]

**Discharge limits recap [V]**
- Alaska: settleable solids **0.2 ml/L**; turbidity **+5 NTU** (drinking-water use, natural <= 50 NTU; 10% increase capped at +25 NTU where natural > 50) or **+25 NTU** (aquatic life) above natural; optional **500-ft mixing zone**; arsenic monitored.
- Yukon: settleable-solids compliance levels by habitat class. Example Moderate-Low: **design 0.2 / action 0.8-1.0 / compliance 1.2-1.5 ml/L**. High-suitability streams are effectively zero discharge.
- Game implication: give each claim a "discharge allowance." Pond capacity (yd3), recycle fraction, and clay content together set the weekly probability of an exceedance. Running past pond capacity late in the season is a classic, foreseeable violation.

## 7. Surprises and common misunderstandings

- **The Yukon royalty is not a tax lever at all.** At C$0.375/oz it is about 0.006% of value. The real Yukon costs are delay (8-18 months), First Nations and wetland conditions, and renewal backlogs.
- **Alaska state claims are larger and cheaper per acre than federal ones**: about $1/acre/yr early on vs $10/acre/yr federal ($200 per 20 acres). But state rent rises 5x with claim age, and state land adds a 3% net-income royalty.
- **Alaska's annual labor has a 5-year cap on cash in lieu.** A pure "paper" claim holder must eventually do real work or drop the ground.
- **Net-income royalties and taxes cost nothing in loss years.** New mines get a 3.5-year license-tax holiday, so the Alaska state take hits only established profitable operators.
- **"Class 3 vs Class 4" is effectively about the wash plant.** 300 m3/day is about 55 gpm, a garden-hose-scale plant.
- **Yukon production is reported in crude ounces, about 80% fine.** Mixing crude and fine ounces overstates revenue by about 25%.
- **Bonds understate real reclamation cost.** Alaska's $750/acre and Idaho's $1,800/acre cap compare with $5-6k/acre actual. Reclamation liability on the balance sheet should use real cost, not bond face.
- **Breakup is getting earlier (about 7 days in a century), but freeze-up variance still decides seasons.** A mild October (2023) added about 3-4 sluicing weeks.
- **Permitting renewal cycles are synchronized** (10-year terms), so whole districts can stall in the same year.
