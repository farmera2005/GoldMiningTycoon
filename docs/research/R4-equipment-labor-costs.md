## Most important numbers (all unverified, from model knowledge)

1. **New iron (2025 USD, typical spec):** 20t excavator ~$290k · 30t ~$430k · 45t ~$680k · 70t+ ~$0.95–1.4M · D6 ~$550k · D8 ~$1.1M · D9 ~$1.6M · D10 ~$2.3M · 30t ADT ~$580k · 40t ADT ~$760k · 966-class loader ~$540k. [U-M]
2. **Used vs new:** dealer-retail used (5–10 yr, 6–12k hr) = **35–60% of new**; tired auction iron (15+ yr, 15k+ hr) = **5–20% of new**; unreserved auction hammer is typically **60–85% of dealer retail** for the same unit. [U-M]
3. **Productivity:** 30t excavator loading trucks **~150–250 bcy/hr** (typical 190) · D8 dozing **~250 bcy/hr at 150 ft**, about **half that at 300 ft** · 40t ADT carries **~24–26 bcy/load** on a 6–12 min cycle. Swell: gravel 1.12–1.25, muck and clayey gravel 1.25–1.40. [U-M]
4. **Fuel:** medium-load burn ≈ **0.015–0.03 gal per rated hp per hour** (load factor 25–55% × ~0.055 gal/bhp-hr): 30t excavator ~6 gph, D8 ~10, D10 ~18, 40t ADT ~7.5. A small northern op (one 30t excavator, D8, loader, 50–75 yd/hr trommel, pump, genset) burns **~400–450 gal/day**, about **55–60k gal per 20-week season**. [U-M / calc]
5. **Diesel 2025–26:** US off-road (dyed) at a regional hub **~$3.0–3.6/gal**. Delivered to a road-accessible AK/YT camp **~$4.0–5.0**. Winter-trail stockpile **~$5–6.5**. Fly-in by air tanker **~$8–15+**. The Canadian consumer carbon charge (21.39 c/L on diesel) ended **2025-04-01**. [U-M]
6. **Repair and maintenance:** mid-life R&M ≈ **5–8% of new price per 1,000 operating hours**, rising roughly linearly with age. Dozer undercarriage lasts **2,000–4,000 hr in abrasive placer gravel** (D8 replacement $60–100k). Engine overhaul at **10–15k hr**. Total operating cost excluding operator for a D8 ≈ **$105–140/hr**. [U-M / U-L]
7. **Tax and ownership:** mining equipment is **MACRS 7-year** (asset class 10.0; the same machine used in construction is 5-year). **100% bonus depreciation was restored permanently** for property acquired after **2025-01-19** (OBBBA, signed 2025-07-04). **§179 limit $2.5M, phase-out from $4.0M** (2025). Equipment insurance **~1–2% of value per year**. [U-H / U-M]
8. **Rental:** monthly rent ≈ **3–4.5% of new price** on a **176-hr single-shift month**; extra hours are billed pro rata, so a 70-hr/week placer schedule pays **~1.5–1.7× the posted monthly**. RPO plans credit **50–80% of rent** toward purchase. [U-M]
9. **Labor (hourly base, 2024–25):** Alaska operator **$38–50**, HD mechanic **$40–55**, laborer **$27–35**. Nevada runs ~15–25% below Alaska. Yukon operator C$30–42 (**US$22–30**). On 66–84 hr weeks, overtime is **30–45% of the wage bill**. Alaska has **daily overtime after 8 hr** (employers with 4+ employees). [U-M]
10. **Burden and camp:** FICA 7.65% + FUTA ~$42 per employee per year + SUTA 1–5.4% up to a ~$42–54k cap + workers' comp **~$3–10 per $100 of payroll** gives **≈1.15–1.30× wages**. Camp costs **$50–120 per person-day**. Small-op all-in cost **~$10–25 per bcy washed** (stripping included), or **~$1,000–2,500 per recovered oz**. Shares: labor 35–45%, fuel 20–30%, R&M and parts 15–25%. [U-L / calc]

## 3. Auctions, used-market behavior and commodity cycles (unverified, from model knowledge)

- **Market structure** [U-M]. RB Global (Ritchie Bros; IronPlanet acquired 2017; IAA acquired 2023) dominates North American heavy-iron auctions. Sales are **unreserved** (no minimum) and **as-is, where-is**. The buyer pays a buyer/transaction fee (mid-single-digit %, capped per lot) plus documentation fees, and must remove the machine within ~2–4 weeks. Seller commissions run ~5–10% (negotiable on large consignments). Inspection reports cover visual condition and run-up only (IronPlanet's "IronClad Assurance" warrants the report, not the machine).
- **Prices** [U-M]. Hammer prices usually land at **60–85% of dealer retail** for comparable units, with wide dispersion. Remote sale sites and low turnout produce bargains, because the buyer bears transport. Units with no maintenance records, failed fluid samples or hidden structural cracks sell 20–40% below comparable units.
- **Cycle behavior** [U-L]:
  - 2013–2016 bust: after the 2011–12 gold and commodity peak, mining fleets (AU, CA, Mongolia, Africa) flooded auctions. Large mining-class dozers and excavators fell **~30–50%** from peak, and smaller construction iron fell ~10–20%.
  - 2021–22: supply-chain shortages pushed used construction-equipment values to records, **+20–40% vs 2019**, unrelated to gold.
  - 2023–25: values normalized downward 10–20% from the peaks as dealer inventory rebuilt.
  - 2024–26 gold run (~$2,000 to $4,000+): demand rose for placer-specific iron (big excavators, D9/D10, wash plants) in Yukon, Alaska and the Guiana Shield. General construction iron tracks construction and interest rates far more than gold.
- **Distress supply** [U-M]. When a regional mine fails, a wave of iron and labor hits the local market. Example: Victoria Gold's Eagle mine heap-leach failure (June 2024) led to receivership (Aug 2024).
- Game implication: usedPrice = retail × (goldIdx)^ε with ε ≈ 0.3–0.5 for placer-specific gear and ≈ 0.1–0.2 for general earthmoving. Auction hammer = retail × U(0.55, 0.9) minus a remoteness discount. Add buyer fee ~6%, as-is hidden condition, and removal within 3 weeks or storage fees.

- **Rule** [U-M]: diesel at load burns ~0.052–0.057 gal per brake-hp-hour, so gph ≈ rated hp × load factor × 0.055. Load factors implied by the medium column: dozer pushing 45–65% (continuous heavy ripping/pushing up to 70%), excavator loading trucks 35–55%, loader feeding 30–45%, ADT hauling 25–40% (empty return and queueing). - **Reference op** [calc]: 30t excavator 66 + D8 110 + 966 loader 60 + trommel 33 + 6" pump 30 + 100 kW genset 24 hr ~120 + pickups ~15 ≈ **430 gal/day**. Over 6.5 days × 20 weeks that is **≈ 56,000 gal (≈ 212,000 L)**, or **≈ 0.85 gal per bcy washed** with stripping included.
- **Lubricants, filters, grease** [U-L]: ≈ 10–15% of fuel cost.
- Game implication: fuel/hr = classHp × loadFactor(task) × 0.055 × wearMultiplier (1.0–1.15). Gensets and pumps burn whenever the plant runs, and camp gensets burn 24/7.

## 8. Depreciation, tax treatment, resale and insurance (unverified, from model knowledge)

- **MACRS class** [U-H]: Rev. Proc. 87-56 asset class **10.0 Mining** = **7-year GDS** (10-year ADS). The same excavator used in construction (class 15.0) is **5-year**. Light and heavy general-purpose trucks (00.241/00.242) are 5-year.
- **7-yr 200% DB half-year table** [U-H]: 14.29 / 24.49 / 17.49 / 12.49 / 8.93 / 8.92 / 8.93 / 4.46%. **5-yr**: 20.00 / 32.00 / 19.20 / 11.52 / 11.52 / 5.76%.
- **Bonus depreciation** [U-H/M]: under TCJA it was 100% (2017–22) then phased down: 80% (2023), 60% (2024), 40% (2025). The **One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21, signed 2025-07-04)** made **100% bonus permanent** for qualified property **acquired after 2025-01-19**. Used property qualifies if it is new to the taxpayer.
- **Section 179** [U-M]: OBBBA set **$2.5M limit, phase-out from $4.0M** for tax years beginning after 2024, inflation-indexed (2026 ≈ $2.56M / $4.09M [U-L]). §179 is limited to business taxable income; bonus is not, so bonus can create an NOL.
- **Recapture trap** [U-H]: selling depreciated equipment triggers **§1245 recapture**, which taxes the gain up to prior depreciation as *ordinary income*. A distressed fleet sale after expensing creates a surprise tax bill in the year cash is shortest.
- **Canada/Yukon** [U-L]: CCA Class 38 (power-operated movable earthmoving equipment) is 30% declining balance. The Accelerated Investment Incentive (1.5× first year) is phasing out 2024–27. Placer gold is a "mineral resource" for CCA purposes.

## 13. Pay structures, hours, overtime law and camp costs (unverified, from model knowledge)

- **Shifts** [U-M]: 1 × 10–12 hr shift, 6–7 days/week for the whole season is normal. Larger ops run 2 × 11–12 hr shifts under the midnight sun (June–July). Rotations are 3-on/1-off weeks or 6-on/1-off days. Fatigue and injury risk climb after ~60 hr/week and after week 8–10 of no days off.
- **U.S. overtime** [U-H]: FLSA requires 1.5× over 40 hr/week, with no miner exemption. Supervisors and managers on salary above the threshold are exempt.
- **Alaska overtime** [U-M]: 1.5× after **8 hr/day or 40 hr/week** for employers with 4+ employees (AS 23.10.060). A voluntary **flexible work-hour plan** filed with DOLWD allows up to 10 hr/day without daily OT. On an 11 × 7 week (77 hr): 40 regular + 37 OT = 95.5 paid-hour equivalents [calc].
- **Nevada overtime** [U-M]: daily OT after 8 hr in 24 applies only to employees earning under 1.5× the minimum wage; most mining hands get weekly OT only.
- **Yukon** [U-L]: OT after 8/day or 40/week at 1.5×, with averaging arrangements possible. Camps often quote a **day rate** (operators C$350–550 per 12-hr day) with room and board included.
- **Pay structures in practice** [U-L]:
  - **Hourly + OT**: most common for crew.
  - **Salary**: foreman, manager, controller, geologist.
  - **Season-completion bonus**: 5–10% of season wages, or $2–10k flat, paid only if the employee stays to freeze-up. Strong anti-quit lever.
  - **Percentage-of-gold**: foreman or key partner 1–5% of gross; whole-crew pool 5–10%. Some family or "grubstake" ops split 50/50 with a claim holder.
  - **Room and board** is included in camp and is a non-cash benefit employees value at ~$40–80/day.
- **Camp cost per person-day** [U-L]: groceries $20–35 at hub prices, plus freight (food ~5–7 lb/person-day × $0.50–1.50/lb fly-in ≈ +$5–10). Self-run all-in (food, propane/power, supplies, laundry, water, waste) **$45–90**, plus cook wages (1 cook per 8–15 people ≈ +$25–50/pd). Contractor-catered industrial camps run **$150–300/pd** (C$150–250).
- **Crew travel** [U-L]: Whitehorse–Dawson scheduled air C$300–600 return. Fairbanks bush charter $1,500–5,000 per load.
- Game implication: pay mix trades cash timing against retention. A completion bonus pushes cash to fall, when gold arrives; percent-of-gold aligns incentives but scales cost with luck. Alaska daily OT makes long shifts ~10–15% pricier than in Nevada.

- **Total burden** [calc]: FICA 7.65% + WC 3–10% + SUTA/FUTA (2–4% early season, falling to ~0 once caps are hit) ≈ **1.15–1.30× wages** for U.S. crew; ~1.12–1.18× in Yukon. Because SUTA and FUTA cap per employee per year, burden is **front-loaded in spring**.
- **MSHA** [per R1]: placer gold mines are metal mines, so **Part 48 Subpart B** applies: 24 hr new-miner training (8 before starting work) and an 8-hr annual refresher, at paid time.
- **Injury rates** [U-L]: metal/nonmetal surface mining total reportable incidence is ~1.5–2.2 per 200,000 hr (≈ per 100 FTE) and lost-time ~0.8–1.2 (MSHA/BLS, 2022–24). Mining fatality rates are ~10–15 per 100k FTE. U.S. mining fatalities: ~40 in 2023, ~20s in 2024. **Powered haulage** (trucks, dozers, loaders) is the largest fatality category, ~40–50%, followed by machinery, falls and electrical. One lost-time injury costs ~$40–100k direct.
- Game implication: injury probability per worker-week = base (~1.9/100 FTE/yr → ~0.04%/40 hr) × hours factor (×1.5 above 60 hr/wk) × fatigue × safety culture × equipment condition. Each claim raises EMR and therefore WC rates for 3 years.

- Old benchmarks: U.S. Bureau of Mines / University of Alaska MIRL studies (1980s–90s) put small Alaska placer costs at roughly $3–8/yd³ in 1990 dollars, ≈ $7–19/yd³ today after ~2.3× inflation [U-L].
- **Cash cost per oz** [U-L/calc]: cost/oz = ($/bcy washed) ÷ (recovered oz/bcy). At $15/bcy and 0.010 oz/bcy recovered = $1,500/oz. At $15 and 0.004 = $3,750/oz, breakeven near $4,000 gold. Small northern ops typically land at **$1,000–2,500/oz cash cost** in 2025 terms.

**Worked reference: integrator's small northern op** [calc, using rates above]. Five employees + owner, 1 × 11 hr × 6.5 days, 20 production weeks + 2 setup weeks, ~45 bcy/hr realized, ~64,000 bcy washed:

| Cost line | Season $ | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Wages: 5 × avg $38/hr × (40 + 31.5 × 1.5) paid-hr × 22 wk × 1.22 burden | ~$445k | ~40% |
| Fuel: ~430 gal/day × 130 days × $4.50 + mob/setup | ~$260k | ~23% |
| R&M, parts, tires/UC, PM: ~$150/hr fleet × 1,430 hr | ~$215k | ~19% |
| Camp: 6 × 150 days × $60 | ~$55k | ~5% |
| Insurance, permits/claim fees, mob/demob, accounting, consumables, freight | ~$150k | ~13% |
| **Operating total (before royalties, debt service, depreciation)** | **~$1.12M ≈ $17.5/bcy** | 100% |

- At 0.012 oz/bcy in pay and 75% recovery: ~575 raw oz ≈ 490 fine oz (0.85 fineness) ≈ **$2.06M at $4,200**. Cash cost ≈ $2,290 per fine oz. A 15% gross lease royalty (~$310k) plus debt service on a $1.5M fleet (~$350–400k/yr) eats most of the ~$0.94M margin. That matches the brief: "royalties and debt service decide the season."
- **Typical shares** [U-L]: labor 35–45% · fuel 20–30% (higher for fly-in, ~35–45%) · R&M and parts 15–25% · camp/travel 5–10% · insurance, admin and permits 5–10%. Royalties (lease 10–20% of gross) and debt service often equal 30–60% of operating cost.
- Game implication: compute $/bcy bottom-up from machine-hours, fuel, wages and parts rather than as a constant. Show the breakeven-grade line on the claim screen.

## 16. Surprising or commonly misunderstood (unverified, from model knowledge)

1. **Plant ratings are loose yd³ of clean feed.** Sustained bank-yard throughput is often 55–75% of nameplate.
2. **"Monthly" rental means 176 hours.** A placer schedule of 70–80 hr/week pays ~1.6–1.8× the posted rate.
3. **MACRS class follows the activity, not the machine.** It is 7-yr in mining, 5-yr in construction. 100% bonus (post 2025-01-19) mostly erases the difference in year 1, but §1245 recapture bites on resale.
4. **Dozer output collapses with distance.** Doubling the push from 150 to 300 ft halves output, so cut geometry is a cost lever.
5. **SMU hours ≠ productive hours.** Idle is 20–40% of meter hours. Wear and PM run on SMU hours; production does not.
6. **Old dozers hold value because they are rebuildable.** A 40-year-old D8K still sells for $20–60k. Wash plants and Tier-3 machines are the opposite.
7. **Alaska has daily overtime**, unlike Nevada and most states. Alaska employees also pay into UI.
8. **SUTA/FUTA cap per employee**, so payroll tax is heaviest in spring, the cash-poorest season.
9. **Remote auctions are cheap for a reason.** The winning bid excludes ~$5–40k of hauling, plus as-is surprises.
10. **The Canadian carbon charge on diesel ended 2025-04-01** (−21.39 c/L ≈ −US$0.58/gal). Pre-2025 Yukon fuel-cost studies overstate today's fuel share.
11. **Tier 4 Final machines fail differently in the cold** (DEF freezing, DPF regens). Old, clean Tier-2/3 iron gets a bush premium.
12. **Yukon reports crude ounces** (~80% fine, per R2). Cost-per-ounce comparisons must use the same ounce basis.
